Design rationale · Home, collection & product page

Built to turn cold traffic into customers

Unpopular You is relaunching with paid Meta traffic: ~70–80% mobile, mostly cold, converting under 1%. Every decision below answers one question: does this help a first-time visitor on a phone understand, trust and buy, fast? The structure follows the Pre-Scale CRO Audit (Glowrate) section by section, and is styled to the brand guidelines.

Brand & system decisions

A
Sitewide

Your palette, with one accent that means something

Colors come straight from your guidelines: Vanilla Mist, Porcelain Mist, Rosy Clay, Clay Taupe, Rose Dust pink and Cocoa Earth, plus the deep cocoa from your Shopify theme for text.

Pink is the brand's signature and the single accent (audit #02): it marks step labels, key badges and highlights, so the eye learns "pink = this matters". Primary buttons use deep cocoa because it gives the strongest contrast for the most important tap, and they fill with pink on hover so the brand color still shows.

B
Sitewide

Fixed 4-step labels everywhere

01 Cleanse · 02 Prep · 03 Correct · 04 Seal appear on every card, the menu, the cart and the product pages in the same order. Four products read as one routine, not four separate decisions, which supports the Core Four and order value.

C
Sitewide

No popups. One 20% offer.

The floating "Join Insider List" is gone (it covered the cart and the Add to Bag). The 40% sale is gone from the hero. There is one offer, 20% off for signing up, and it lives in three calm places: the announcement bar, a small box next to Add to Bag, and the footer. The signup only opens when someone taps it.

D
Sitewide

Plain-English line under every product name

"Rooted Radiance Brightening Serum" is a beautiful name, but a cold visitor needs "a brightening serum that targets the look of dark spots while it hydrates". Every card, menu row and product page carries that one line (audit #03).

E
Sitewide

Visual menu and cart drawer

The mobile menu shows all four products with image, step and one-liner, then the bundle, and ends on "Nurse-founded · Made for melanin-rich skin" (audit #23). The cart shows a free-shipping progress bar to $60, a clearly readable savings line and a Complete your routine block listing only the steps the shopper doesn't have yet (audit #22).

F
Motion

Animation that guides, never blocks

GSAP is used for calm, premium motion: the hero image opens like a reveal, headlines rise word by word, the two traditions slide together, the Barrier First statement lights up as you scroll, stats count up, and items fly into the bag. Nothing auto-plays sound or auto-rotates content the shopper is reading, and everything switches off for people who prefer reduced motion.

Home page: section by section

01
Announcement bar

Real announcements, slow enough to read

Three messages only (20% signup, free shipping over $60, Blissim feature), rotating every 6 seconds with arrows. Pauses on hover.

02
Hero · audit #04

Who it's for and why it's different, in 3 seconds

"Skincare for melanin-rich skin that glows" names the audience and the outcome. The subheading says what's different (Jamaican botanicals + Korean-inspired gentleness, barrier-first). One CTA, "Shop the routine", plus three trust points: Nurse-founded · Made for melanin-rich skin · 20,000+ community. On mobile the image sits on top with copy and CTA right under it, so the buy path is above the fold.

03
Press · audit #05

Quotes, not just logos

One publication at a time with what they actually said, led by Blissim's real line ("a hydration bath for the most sensitive skin"). Swipe or tap; it never moves on its own. Glamour, Vogue and Tatler quotes are placeholders until you confirm the exact wording.

04
Two traditions · audit #06

The reason to choose you over a bigger brand

Jamaican botanicals (soursop, moringa, guava leaf, pumpkin) + Korean-inspired science (rice ferment, ginseng, ceramides, peptides, niacinamide), joined by Barrier First. Three short steps explain the benefit. It uses your real ingredient lists, which makes the claim believable.

05
Values · audit #09

Credentials in one glance

Cruelty-free · Made in the USA · Fragrance-free · Suitable for sensitive skin · Nurse-founded. A slow pink band that also adds brand color.

06
Build your routine · audit #07

Replaces "bestsellers"

With four products, a bestsellers grid looks thin. A swipeable 4-step carousel (2 per screen on mobile) shows step, rating, one-liner, price and size, with Add to bag directly on the card for decided shoppers and a clickable name for researchers. A bundle nudge sits right under it.

07
Routine map · audit #08

Our answer to the quiz question

With four products, a multi-step quiz adds clicks without adding much. Instead, shoppers tap their concern (dark spots, sensitivity, dullness, dryness) and the matching steps light up with "Start here". You get the benefit of a quiz in 10 seconds and one tap. If you'd still like a full quiz with email capture, we can add one later.

08
Philosophy

Barrier First, said plainly

You asked to promote the Barrier First philosophy. It appears in the hero badge, the traditions section and the values band, and here as one statement that lights up word by word as you scroll.

09
UGC video · audit #11

Real hands, real skin

Your own videos are used, tap to play, muted with captions, about 1.5 cards visible on mobile so it's clear there are more. Each video links to the product it shows. We'll swap in community clips (with permission) as you collect them.

10
Founder · audit #10

Shauna, the nurse, is the trust

Placed after the products and proof have done their work: story, quote, credentials, "Read our story". The photo is a still from your video for now; a warm portrait of you will make it even stronger.

11
Results · audit #12

Honest, compliant proof

Labelled as a consumer perception study with the source line underneath (FTC/ASA safe). Action needed: the old figures came from the Papaya serum study, so they must either sit on the tested product or be replaced with a study of the new formulas.

12
Reviews · audit #13

Selected, specific, linked

One review at a time, with first name and skin concern, and a product card under each one that links to the product. While review numbers are still low, leading with specific praise looks stronger than a big widget showing "10 reviews".

13
Bundle · audit #14

Sell the solution, then the saving

The headline is the problem it solves ("fade the look of dark spots and strengthen your barrier"); the $24 saving supports it. Each step is listed with its separate price, so the value is obvious without shouting "sale".

14
FAQ · audit #24

Handle objections at 2am

The 8 questions cold buyers ask (who it's for, dark spots, sensitivity, results timing, returns…). This also helps you show up in AI search answers.

Collection & product page

C
Collection · audit #21

Clean, consistent, shoppable

No borders. Same-shape images. Every card shows step, rating, title, one-liner, price and size, with a second image on hover and quick add. The Core Four leads as a wide feature card because it's the product you most want to sell. Concern filters and a sort sit in a sticky bar. A 4-step guide underneath explains the routine.

P1
Product · audit #15–#17

Above the fold, in buying order

Same-shape gallery with thumbnails → rating → title → one-liner → price with size → four tick bullets (result · melanin-rich · gentle · ingredients) → one-tap upgrade to the Core Four → Add to bag → shipping/secure/guarantee microcopy → payment icons → 20% signup box. No breadcrumb link leading away from the page. On mobile, a sticky Add to bag bar is always within reach.

P2
Product · audit #18–#20

Below the fold, answering the next question

Values strip → accordions (Description, How to use, Ingredients, Shipping) → annotated ingredient spotlight (thin lines from ingredient names to the real product photo, the premium version of the old infographics) → Complete the Routine showing "You're here" in the 4-step order with an upgrade to the Core Four → product FAQ + video → reviews → related products.

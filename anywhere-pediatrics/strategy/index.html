Home page redesign · Strategy & rationale

A home page built to turn South Florida parents into members

Every section, its position and its wording has one job: move a worried parent from "what is this?" to "I want this kit" with as little friction as possible. This page explains why each piece is where it is.

1. Understood in 5 secondsWhat it is, who it's for and why it beats a normal video visit, all above the fold.
2. Trusted in 30 secondsLicensed doctors, real parent stories, transparent pricing and clear safety messaging.
3. Acted onOne primary action ("Get Your Kit") repeated at every decision point, plus a low-pressure call-back form.

What's holding the current site back

The current Wix site has strong raw material: a genuinely different product, real testimonials and honest pricing. The problems are in structure, hierarchy and trust, and they cost conversions.

Issue The hero doesn't sell the difference

The headline is written for SEO ("Pediatric Telehealth in South Florida…") rather than for a parent. The key differentiator, a real exam and not just a video call, appears lower down.

Issue Long text blocks before any proof

Two dense paragraphs come before the steps, the kit and the reviews. Parents scan; they don't read essays.

Issue Mixed signals around buying

"Membership", "lease" and "Buy Now" all describe the same thing, and the card fee and per-visit cost show up late. Surprises at checkout cause drop-off.

Issue Weak trust and safety signals

There's no visible emergency guidance, no FAQ to handle objections (insurance, ages, ownership) and testimonials sit far below the fold.

Issue An 8-field contact form

Every extra required field lowers completion. The current form asks for 8 required fields before a parent can ask a single question.

Issue No mobile conversion path

Most parents will arrive on a phone, but there's no sticky call or buy button and no tap-to-call in the header.

The new page flow

The order follows a proven decision path for a considered purchase: Attention → Problem → Solution → Proof → Price → Objections → Action.

  1. Hero
  2. Trust strip
  3. Problem & comparison
  4. How it works
  5. The kit
  6. Before / after
  7. Reviews
  8. Pricing & calculator
  9. FAQ
  10. Final CTA & form
  11. Service area

Section by section: what it does and why it's there

01 · Top bar, header & hero

Win the first 5 seconds

  • Benefit-led headline: "Real pediatric exams at home. Not just a video call." names the category and the difference in one line.
  • Supporting copy lists exactly what's checked (ears, throat, heart, lungs…) and the key promise: a doctor reviews results before the visit.
  • One primary CTA in the only purple button style on the page, plus a soft secondary ("See how it works") for parents not ready to buy.
  • Micro-commitments under the button (from $99/mo, up to 5 kids, ages 1 to 21) answer the first three questions before they're asked.
  • Floating "result" cards on the photo show the product working: lung sounds recorded, SpO₂ 98%, doctor reviewed.
  • Phone number and "Emergency? Call 911" in the top bar: instant credibility and responsible medical messaging.

Tell the client: "A parent landing here knows within one glance what you do, why it's better than other telehealth, and what it costs to start."

02 · Trust strip

Remove the biggest fears early

  • US-licensed physicians, prescriptions to any pharmacy, secure sharing and a travel-ready kit: the four reassurances parents look for in telehealth.
  • It sits right under the hero, where hesitation peaks after the first scroll.
03 · Problem + comparison table

Make the pain real, then show the better option

  • Three relatable moments (the late-night fever, the urgent-care Saturday, the pre-flight ear infection) taken from your own testimonials, so parents think "that's me".
  • The comparison table places Anywhere Pediatrics against urgent care and video-only telehealth. It's the only option with a check in every row, and that justifies the price before the price appears.

Tell the client: "Your real competitor isn't another kit, it's 'I'll just go to urgent care'. This table wins that argument visually."

04 · How it works

Make it feel easy

  • Examine → Share → Connect, each with a real photo of a parent using the device. Seeing a parent do it reduces the "I can't do medical stuff" fear.
  • A CTA right after the steps catches parents who are already convinced.
05 · The kit

Show the value in the box

  • The annotated kit image plus all 9 guided exams as scannable cards, instead of a long paragraph.
  • The headline "Your doctor gets the full picture before you say hello" turns features into a parent benefit.
06 · Before / after

Contrast sells

  • The old way is crossed out and the new way is checked. It reuses your "No more…" lines in a format that's far easier to scan.
  • A dark band breaks the page rhythm and resets attention halfway down.
07 · Reviews

Social proof right before the price

  • Your 4 real testimonials, with the travel story featured large because it shows the most unique benefit.
  • Each one carries a name, child's age and city, which makes it more believable and locally relevant.
  • Placed directly before pricing, so trust is at its peak when the cost appears.
08 · Pricing + family cost calculator

Transparent pricing that feels like good value

  • Shown as $99/month (anchored price) with $1,188 upfront stated clearly underneath, so the full cost is never hidden.
  • The kit is highlighted as the main plan; the $99 visit is clearly "only when you need it".
  • The interactive calculator shows the cost per child per month. With 3 kids and 4 visits the cost works out to about $44 per child per month. Interaction also keeps visitors engaged.
  • The card fee is disclosed up front, so there are no checkout surprises.

Tell the client: "Families with several kids are your best customers. The calculator makes that value obvious without any discounting."

09 · FAQ

Answer objections before they become exits

  • Covers training, ages, "does it replace my pediatrician?", insurance, travel, ownership and emergencies.
  • Phone and email sit right beside it, so a parent with one last question can reach a human.
10 · Final CTA + 2-step call-back form

Two ways to say yes

  • Ready buyers get "Get Your Kit Now". Hesitant parents get "Have a question? We'll call you."
  • 2-step form: step 1 asks only for name, phone and email. Starting is easy, and once someone starts they tend to finish. Step 2 collects children, city and reason using one-tap buttons.
  • The privacy and emergency note is kept, but it's short and friendly.
11 · Service area + footer

Keep the local SEO, lose the clutter

  • All 28 cities from the current site, kept for local search ranking but shown as tidy tags near the bottom, where they don't distract buyers.
  • The footer repeats contact details, the corrected address ("Suite 300") and a clear emergency disclaimer.

Built for phones first

Parents usually look for help on their phone, often with a sick child on their lap.

  • Sticky bottom bar with "Call" and "Get Your Kit · $99/mo" appears after the hero and hides near the form, so the next step is always one thumb-tap away.
  • Full-width buttons, large tap targets and a simple menu.
  • The comparison table is redesigned to fit a phone screen, with no sideways scrolling.
  • Tap-to-call phone links everywhere.
  • Tested at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440px wide.

CRO features at a glance

One consistent CTA

"Get Your Kit" in a single purple style, repeated 8 times at each decision point.

Price anchoring

$99/month first, the full $1,188 stated honestly, and a per-child cost in the calculator.

Social proof

Stars and avatars in the hero, plus full testimonials placed just before pricing.

Objection handling

The comparison table, a 7-question FAQ and human contact options.

Low-friction lead capture

3 fields to start (down from 8 required), with a progress bar and inline validation.

Trust & safety

US-licensed physicians, a secure-sharing message and visible emergency guidance.

Speed & accessibility

Plain HTML/CSS/JS, lazy-loaded images, keyboard-friendly, reduced-motion support.

Subtle motion

GSAP scroll reveals and a price count-up that add polish without slowing the page.

Easy to test

Every CTA is tagged (data-cta), ready for Google Analytics or Hotjar click tracking and A/B tests.

Denial letter decoder

What your insurer said — and what it often means.

Pick the phrase from your letter. These are the reasons we see most — and most are worth a second look.

Have an attorney read my letter — free

What they wrote

"The damage observed is consistent with long-term wear and deterioration."

What it often means

The adjuster attributed storm damage to age — often after a short inspection, sometimes from the ground or by drone only, and without an engineer.

What we check

  • Weather data for the storm date at your address
  • Independent roofer & engineer findings
  • Photos and condition before the storm

What they wrote

"The claimed damage pre-dates the reported date of loss."

What it often means

The insurer is shifting the timeline so the loss falls outside the policy period — frequently without evidence showing when the damage actually happened.

What we check

  • Prior inspections, sale or refinance reports
  • Storm reports and satellite imagery
  • Whether the insurer's own file supports the claim

What they wrote

"The estimated cost of repair does not exceed your deductible."

What it often means

Their estimate left things out — interior damage, code upgrades, matching materials, or whole areas of the property — so the number came in low.

What we check

  • Line-by-line estimate comparison
  • Missed rooms, systems and code items
  • Independent contractor pricing

What they wrote

"This loss is excluded under Section I – Exclusions of your policy."

What it often means

An exclusion is being read more broadly than its actual language — or applied to a loss caused by a covered peril like wind or hail.

What we check

  • The exact policy wording and endorsements
  • Cause-of-loss evidence
  • How your state's courts read that clause

What they wrote

"The loss was not reported within a reasonable time."

What it often means

Late-notice denials are common after hail and wind events, when damage isn't visible from the ground. Many states require the insurer to show the delay actually hurt them.

What we check

  • When the damage was discoverable
  • Your state's notice and prejudice rules
  • Communications with your agent or carrier

Straight answers

Questions policyholders ask us first.

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing upfront. We work on a contingency fee — if we don't recover money for you, you don't pay us a fee. The exact terms are spelled out in your engagement letter before you sign anything.

My claim was denied. Is it too late to do anything?

Usually not. A denial is the insurer's position, not a final ruling. Many denied claims are reopened, re-evaluated, or resolved after independent evidence is presented. Deadlines do apply and vary by state — that's why it's worth a free review now.

What is insurance bad faith?

Insurers owe you a duty to investigate honestly, decide promptly and pay fairly. When an insurer denies without a reasonable investigation, delays without cause, misrepresents the policy, or underpays a covered loss, that may be bad faith — and many states give policyholders additional remedies.

Can I still get more if I already accepted a payment?

Often, yes. Cashing a check doesn't always end your claim — it depends on the release language and your state's rules. Before you sign anything marked "full and final," let us look at it.

How long will my claim take?

It depends on the damage, the insurer, and whether litigation is needed. Many claims resolve through negotiation or mediation in months, not years. We contact your insurer within 24 hours to get things moving immediately.

Do you handle commercial properties too?

Yes. We represent homeowners, businesses, landlords and multifamily owners, and HOA and condo associations on residential and commercial property claims.

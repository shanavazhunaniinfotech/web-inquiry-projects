Homepage concept · design rationale

THE LINE. ONE STORY, ONE PRODUCT, ONE SCROLL.

Why every element on the proposed homepage sits where it does, how it follows the Surecheck brand book, and how it turns visitors into buyers.

← Back to the homepage concept

The big idea

The brand book defines the Surecheck Stripe as the control line on the test, "a symbol of accuracy". We made that line the thread of the whole homepage. It draws itself in the intro, cuts diagonally through the hero, fills the left edge as a reading-progress bar, and finally shows up as the result lines in the test window.

The Streamer itself is the narrator. One product travels down the page and changes as the story moves. It tilts into the hero, lies flat across the giant type, drops into the product card, stands upright while the 3-step guide plays, shows its result lines when you learn to read them, and comes back for the final call to action. As you asked, the page is one continuous scene rather than separate blocks. The background colour also blends from section to section, so there is never a hard cut.

Marvis-level motion, not Marvis's look

We took the approach from the reference (continuous movement, the product as hero, kinetic type, smooth transitions) and built it entirely from Surecheck's own palette, typography and stripe system.

Neutral by design

The brand book says Surecheck "does not gravitate towards being pregnant or otherwise". The copy never assumes the answer anyone hopes for ("Whatever you're hoping for…"), and result explanations are calm and factual. That is why the double stripe only appears next to the single stripe.

Brand book compliance

Surecheck Red #B81466: CTAs, stripe, headlines Violet #684286: secondary, dark chapters Lavender #E4DDEB: base canvas Cassette Pink: Cassette only
  • Tagline "ACCURATE AT ALL TIMES" is the hero headline. It uses the approved weight pairing (ExtraBold + Light, caps), sits next to the logo in the header, and repeats in the closing marquee and footer.
  • Typography: titles are ExtraBold/Black plus Light in caps, body copy is Light/Regular at 16px or more, and text is ranged left, all as in section 2.02. The prototype uses Figtree, a free stand-in. Production will use Proxima Nova via Adobe Fonts.
  • Logo: full colour on light backgrounds and white monochrome in the dark footer. It is never rotated, stretched, given 3D effects or shadows, and it keeps its clear space.
  • Stripes: the single stripe is always Surecheck Red. The double stripe never appears on its own.

Section by section: what & why

  1. Intro + hero: "Accurate at all times."

    A 1.5-second intro draws the control line, then the hero opens with the tagline, a short neutral promise (3 minutes, over 99%, first day of a missed period) and the Streamer tilting in over the red stripe.

    CRO: the value proposition, the proof and two CTAs are all above the fold. "Find Surecheck near you" is the primary action and "When can I test?" is the secondary one for visitors who aren't ready yet. Trust chips (accuracy, 3 min, any time of day, CE) answer the first worries straight away. The intro plays only once per session so it never slows down returning visitors.

  2. Statement: product meets typography

    Giant kinetic type ("ONE LINE · TWO LINES / ZERO GUESSWORK") slides in opposite directions while the product lies flat between the rows. Below it is the 20-years heritage line.

    Why here: this is the emotional hook and the brand moment the brief asks for, placed right after the promise. It turns "accuracy" into something you can see before we ask for anything.

  3. The tests: Streamer vs Cassette

    Two product cards with a one-tap "I'd rather…" chooser that highlights the right test. Each card has benefits, a "Where to buy" button and a "How to use" link.

    CRO: product choice comes early so visitors with buying intent can convert at the third screen. The chooser takes the decision off the visitor and avoids choice paralysis. The flying product lands in the Streamer card, which links the story to the product itself.

  4. How to use: pinned 3-step story

    The section pins while you scroll. The product turns upright, each step lights up, a 3-minute reading timer runs and the control line appears in the window.

    Why: this is the interactive instructions from the brief. Showing how simple it is removes the "will I do it wrong?" barrier, which is a major purchase objection for first-time buyers. On mobile it becomes simple stacked steps so it is never cramped.

  5. How to read: interactive result reader

    Visitors tap Pregnant / Faint line / Not pregnant / Invalid. The close-up window and the product's own lines update, each with a calm explanation. Without interaction it cycles through the results automatically.

    Why: result interpretation is the most-searched topic in this category. Answering it clearly ("a faint line still means pregnant") builds medical credibility and makes Surecheck the trusted source, so it is the test people come back to and buy.

  6. When to test: calculator

    This is the existing site's calculator, redesigned. It takes the last period date and cycle length, or uses the "19 days after conception" rule for irregular cycles. A live cycle strip shows the period, fertile window and test day. The result includes "Add to my calendar" (.ics) and "Get Surecheck ready".

    CRO: this is the strongest micro-conversion for visitors who aren't ready to buy yet. It gives them a date and a calendar reminder, then asks them to buy before that date, which turns "not yet" into "planned". The product leaves the screen here so nothing distracts from the form.

  7. Why it's accurate: Colloidal Gold

    A full Surecheck Red chapter. Gold particles converge into a single line as you scroll, explaining the technology visually. Counters show over 99%, 3 min, 20 years and CE, with a link to the real certificate.

    Why here: proof is placed after the visitor has engaged with the tools and before the softer brand content, which is exactly when the question "can I trust it?" comes up.

  8. Campaign + community stories

    A flexible campaign slot (now the 20-year campaign with your own photography) with "Share your story", followed by three short community quotes.

    Why: the brief asks for an easily updated campaign area. Peer stories are the most persuasive social proof for Gen Z and Millennials. The quotes are placeholders, to be replaced with real stories.

  9. The Surecheck Journal

    A drag or swipe rail of your real articles with category filters (Love & dating, Mind, Body & science), ending in a link to the full Journal and Sex-Ed hub.

    Why: editorial content keeps the brand present between purchases and supports SEO. The rail shows a lot of content without making the page longer.

  10. FAQs

    Your six most important FAQs in an accordion that keeps one answer open at a time.

    CRO: this handles the last objections (medication, timing, pale lines) right before the final call to action.

  11. Final CTA: "Ready when you are."

    The product returns on violet with "Find a pharmacy" and "Buy online". Both open a "Where to buy" drawer with a country selector, partner shops and a pharmacy finder.

    CRO: the story ends where the purchase happens. The drawer keeps people on the page instead of sending them away, and every "Where to buy" button on the page leads to the same place.

  12. Footer + newsletter

    A GDPR-ready newsletter sign-up ("Join the conversation"), the full site structure, the language switcher and a medical-device disclaimer.

Conversion toolkit

Always-available CTA

"Where to buy" is in the header. On mobile, a sticky bottom bar with "When to test?" and "Where to buy" appears after the hero.

Two conversion paths

Ready buyers go to Where to buy. Not-yet visitors go to the calculator, a calendar reminder and the newsletter. Nobody hits a dead end.

Objection handling in order

Promise → product choice → "Is it easy?" → "Can I read it?" → "When?" → "Can I trust it?" → FAQs → buy.

Measurable

Every CTA, calculator use, result-reader tap, drawer opening and country selection can be tracked as a GA4 event to keep optimising after launch.

Motion, accessibility & performance

  • GSAP + ScrollTrigger + Lenis handle the smooth, scroll-driven movement. The product is vector or 3D, so it stays sharp at every size.
  • Reduced motion: visitors with "reduce motion" switched on get a calm, static version with the same content and tools.
  • On mobile, long product moves fade through instead of crossing text. Reading always comes first, as the brief asks for medical information.
  • The page has keyboard navigation, visible focus states, an ARIA-labelled result reader and drawer, and AA contrast on all text.
  • Images are lazy-loaded and there is no heavy video in the first view. The target is a Lighthouse performance score of 90+ on mobile.

Version B: "Clinical Light"

Version B has the same content, tools and conversion logic as Version A, presented in a completely different design. Version A is bold and colourful, with the product flying between colour blocks. Version B is clean, white and editorial, with lab-like precision: fine grid lines, mono "lab label" type and lots of space.   1. Loader: "Calibrating accuracy" 00 → 99%

    A short count-up turns the main claim (over 99%) into the first thing a visitor experiences. It plays only once per session.

  2. Hero + product anatomy: one pinned scene

    "KNOW. FOR SURE." with the Streamer passing between the words and spec callouts (accuracy, result window, legend, any time of day). As you scroll, the headline clears and the same product turns upright and leads a four-chapter tour: the window (zoom, control line appears), the legend (zoom on the printed guide), Colloidal Gold (gold particles form the line in the window) and use anywhere, which ends with "Get the Streamer".

    Why: this delivers the client's "product moving and transforming through one continuous narrative" in the most literal way. The product explains itself, which builds credibility before we ask for anything. Both buttons are above the fold from the first second.

  3. Manifesto

    The brand promise lights up word by word as you read, with the key words in Surecheck Red, followed by the single Surecheck Stripe.

  4. Choose your test

    A Streamer/Cassette toggle in a lab-grid "specimen" stage, with spec rows (accuracy, method, result, when) and a "Where to buy" button.

    CRO: a side-by-side spec table removes doubt for comparison shoppers and keeps the purchase one click away.

  5. How to use: horizontal scroll

    A dark chapter where four step cards slide sideways and a real 3-minute timer ring fills as you scroll. On mobile it becomes a swipeable carousel.

  6. Read it right

    A large, centred result window with Pregnant / Faint / Not pregnant / Invalid tabs. It has the same calm, neutral explanations as Version A.

  7. When to test + proof bento

    The calculator (with cycle dots, calendar reminder and "Get Surecheck ready") sits next to the proof tiles: over 99%, 3 min, 20 years, CE (linked to the certificate) and any time of day.

    CRO: visitors use the tool and see the proof in the same view. The reassurance sits right beside the decision.

  8. Campaign: expands to full screen

    The campaign photo grows from a framed card to full bleed as you scroll, then "20 years. Millions of stories." and "Share your story" appear, followed by the community quotes.

  9. The Journal

    An editorial index with a featured article and a list. On desktop, an image preview follows the cursor. Category filters are included.

  10. FAQs → "Ready when you are."

    Numbered FAQs with a "talk to your doctor or pharmacist" note, then a dark, glowing final call to action with the product, "Find a pharmacy" and "Buy online", which open the same where-to-buy drawer.

Details: custom cursor with labels ("Buy", "Read", "View"), magnetic buttons, a section indicator, a sticky mobile CTA bar that appears after the opening scene, a reduced-motion fallback and full keyboard support.

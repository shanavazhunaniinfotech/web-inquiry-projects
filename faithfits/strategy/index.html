Home page concept · Design rationale

Built to turn visitors into customers.

FaithFits already has the hardest things to copy: beautiful product photography, a meaningful mission and a verse on every sleeve. The new home page keeps all of it and puts it in the order a shopper needs to say yes. Every section below has one job.

Faster to productWomen / Men split in the hero and a Quick Add on every card: from landing to cart in 3 taps.
Stronger reasons to buyFabric, fit, reviews and a comparison answer “why $120?” before the shopper has to ask.
The brand's unfair advantageEach color is named for a promise. We made choosing a color feel like choosing a verse.

What we found on the current home page

  • TodayHero says “Wear your faith” with one generic “Shop collection” button.
    NewA clear promise (who it's for and why it's different) plus separate Shop Women / Shop Men buttons.
  • TodayEvery product reads “Sale price $120”, but nothing is on sale, which quietly hurts trust.
    NewClean “$120 · top + pants” pricing that sells the set value instead.
  • Today“Choose options” sends shoppers off to another page to pick sizes.
    NewQuick Add with separate top and pants sizes, right on the home page, then a slide-out cart.
  • TodayNo reviews, ratings or customer voices anywhere on the page.
    NewRating in the hero, on every card, and a reviews section showing each reviewer's role (RN, CNA, Paramedic).
  • TodayA countdown sitting at 00:00:00 and a blank “Wear your faith” banner.
    NewRemoved. Empty or broken elements make a store look abandoned.
  • TodayThe scripture sleeve, the strongest differentiator, is barely shown.
    NewA close-up “Look closer” section with tappable hotspots, plus the verse on every product card.
  • TodayStory and commitments come before shoppers know if the scrubs are good.
    NewProduct proof first, then the mission, so the story turns a purchase into belonging.

Section order, and why

  1. Announcement barFree US shipping and 15% off first order are seen before anything else, removing the two most common reasons to hesitate.
  2. HeroPromise, Women / Men buttons, star rating and free shipping in one screen. Real FaithFits photography with color tags introduces the product names.
  3. Trust stripShipping, stretch, anti-microbial and scripture: four buying questions answered in four seconds.
  4. Find your fit (Women · Men · Wide Leg)A second, visual path into the catalogue. Wide Leg is flagged NEW to push the newest collection.
  5. Best sellers with Quick AddTabs by category, hover to see a second photo, color dots, the verse reference and one-tap sizing. Shorter path = more orders.
  6. Find your calling colorThe signature, interactive moment: tap a color, see the verse and the photo change, add to cart from right there. No other scrub brand can do this.
  7. Look closerOnline shoppers can't touch fabric. Close-up photography and specs (72/21/7 blend, 4-way stretch, anti-microbial, pockets) replace the fitting room.
  8. Reviews by roleA nurse trusts a nurse. Showing the reviewer's job makes social proof far more persuasive.
  9. Comparison tableAnswers “why not cheaper scrubs?” without naming competitors.
  10. Our story + prayerThe FaithFits prayer runs as a moving line and the mission follows, once the shopper already trusts the product.
  11. Fit finder + size chart“Will it fit?” is the biggest reason apparel shoppers leave. Two quick questions give a size.
  12. FAQFaithFits' own six questions, answered on the page.
  13. Email capture (15% off)Visitors not ready today join the list, so Klaviyo flows can bring them back.
  14. Final call to action“Your shift. His strength.” with the same Women / Men buttons, so no one has to scroll back up.

Always-on conversion helpers

  1. Sticky header with live cart countNavigation and cart are always one tap away.
  2. Slide-out cart with “Complete your rotation”Suggests a second color, since healthcare workers need several sets a week. Raises average order value.
  3. Sticky Shop bar on mobileAppears after the hero so the next step is always visible on phones, where most traffic is.
  4. Welcome offer, shown politelyOnly after 30 seconds or when leaving, once per visit, never on arrival.
  5. Color searchShoppers usually know the color their hospital requires. Typing “navy” finds it instantly.

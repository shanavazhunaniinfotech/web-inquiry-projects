Armed conflict & consequences

The cost of war is seven times what the world spends on it.

In 2024, global military spending reached $2.72 trillion. That does not include $14–17 trillion in additional costs — loss of life, damage to property, disability and economic losses — all largely borne by civilians living in conflict areas.

01 — Our Mission

“To establish a global convention and treaty defending the rights of civilians impacted by armed conflict to reparations.”

02 — Our Goals

Awareness & compliance

Build greater awareness of and compliance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law as a means for protection and peace building in conflict settings, focusing on civilians and actors locally engaged.

Political will

Build political will to make existing standards on reparations and associated remedies a reality for civilians in conflict-riven regions across the globe.

Reparations as a norm

Establish reparations and associated remedies as legal, moral, and cultural norms that address humanitarian needs, heal deep-seated grievances, and restore justice.

Connecting people

Link civilians impacted by armed conflict, progressive individuals, governments, organizations, field practitioners in civil society, NGOs, and human rights organizations to expertise to advocate and assist in promoting and developing programs.

03 — Our People

Founder

Steve Hollingworth

Our campaign was founded by Steve Hollingworth, an activist and author. Steve has worked with humanitarian efforts and has seen the effects of armed conflict around the world.

Funded by Re:Imagine, an Atlanta-based organization for young creatives, the campaign began in February 2026.
